## why-keigo-hard-title
Why is keigo hard for bilingual speakers?

## why-keigo-hard
Most Japanese classes teach one rule: use honorific forms (尊敬語) for people above you, and humble forms (謙譲語) for yourself. That rule works in everyday conversation.

Business Japanese has a second axis: ウチ (in-group) and ソト (out-group). When 田中, your manager, takes a call from a client, 田中 is senior to you inside the company. But to the client, 田中 belongs to your group (ウチ). When you refer to 田中's actions to that client, you use humble forms, not honorific ones, because 田中 is ウチ relative to the client's ソト.

This ウチ/ソト distinction is what most textbooks leave out, and it is the source of most keigo mistakes in professional settings.
